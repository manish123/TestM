# What is Panchang?

Panchang, also written Panchanga, is a traditional Hindu calendrical system used to describe and organize time through several interrelated measures. The word is commonly explained as “five limbs”, referring to five principal elements: Tithi, Vara, Nakshatra, Yoga and Karana.

A Panchang is therefore more than a list of dates. It is a time-information system. It combines calendrical and astronomical measures to describe a particular day and the periods within it.

## Why does Panchang exist?

A civil calendar answers questions such as “What date is it?” A Panchang provides additional information about the character and structure of a day according to traditional Indian calendrical practice.

Historically, such information has been used to determine observances, festivals, fasting days, ritual timings and auspicious periods. Different traditions and regional Panchanga systems can apply different rules for particular observances, so a Panchang result should always be understood in its calendrical context.

The practical idea is simple: before deciding when to perform an activity, understand the time being selected.

## How is Panchang calculated?

The five principal Panchanga elements are derived from astronomical and calendrical quantities:

- **Tithi** is based on the angular relationship between the Moon and Sun.
- **Vara** is the weekday.
- **Nakshatra** identifies the lunar mansion occupied by the Moon.
- **Yoga** is derived from the combined solar and lunar longitudes.
- **Karana** is a half-Tithi division used in calendrical calculation.

The exact beginning and ending of these periods depend on astronomical positions and, for the displayed civil time, the observer's location and time zone. Sunrise and sunset are also important for many traditional day-based rules and observances.

A Panchang engine therefore cannot be treated as a fixed table that is identical everywhere. The same astronomical event may be represented at different local clock times in different locations.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditional Panchanga practice uses these measures to determine festival dates, fasting observances, ritual periods and Muhurta. The five limbs are not interpreted as five independent predictions; together they describe the calendrical context of a time.

Interpretation also varies by tradition. A festival rule, fasting rule or Muhurta rule may depend on a particular Tithi at sunrise, a specified period during the day, or another rule defined by the relevant tradition.

Panchang should therefore be understood as a framework for selecting and understanding time, rather than as a claim that every moment mechanically produces a particular outcome.

## How should Panchang be used?

A practical use of Panchang is to answer four questions:

1. **What is happening in the calendar?** — Tithi, Nakshatra, festival, Vrat and other observances.
2. **When does it happen here?** — Calculate the relevant period for the user's location.
3. **What does the tradition recommend?** — Consult the applicable observance or Muhurta rule.
4. **What should I do?** — Make the decision yourself, using Panchang as timing information rather than as an automatic decision-maker.

For everyday use, a person can start with a few concepts such as Tithi, Rahu Kaal and Abhijit Muhurta and gradually learn how the larger Panchanga system fits together.

## Common misconceptions

### Panchang is the same as a horoscope

No. Panchang primarily describes calendrical and time-related information. A horoscope is a chart or interpretive system based on planetary positions for a particular person or event.

### There is one universally identical Panchang time for everyone

No. Location matters. Sunrise, sunset, time zone and local astronomical circumstances affect displayed timings.

### Panchang tells you what will happen

Panchang is primarily a system for describing and selecting time. Traditional users may attach significance to particular periods, but the calendar itself should not be presented as a deterministic prediction of events.

### Every festival has one globally fixed civil date

Not necessarily. Festival determination can depend on the relevant traditional rule, location and local day boundaries. This is one reason location-aware Panchang calculation matters.
