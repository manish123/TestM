# What is Amavasya?

Amavasya is the Tithi associated with the end of Krishna Paksha and the new-moon portion of the lunisolar month. It is a calendrical interval defined through the Sun-Moon relationship, not simply the civil date on which the Moon is visually absent.

## Why does Amavasya exist?

Amavasya is a recurring lunar-calendar boundary and is associated with many observances, including regional practices for worship, remembrance and fasting.

## How is Amavasya calculated?

Amavasya is determined through the Tithi system from the angular separation of the Sun and Moon. Its exact beginning and ending time are calculated from astronomical positions and expressed in local civil time.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditional observances associated with Amavasya vary significantly by region, family and tradition. Some practices emphasise remembrance of ancestors, while others focus on particular deities, fasting or ritual observance.

## How should it be used?

Use the local Tithi timing and the specific observance rule rather than relying only on the visual phase of the Moon or a generic calendar date.

## Common misconceptions

### Amavasya is simply the night when no Moon is visible

It is more precise to treat it as a Tithi defined astronomically; visibility and calendrical boundaries are not identical concepts.

### Every Amavasya has identical observances

No. Practices differ by lunar month and tradition.
