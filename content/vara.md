# What is Vara?

Vara is the weekday component of Panchang. The seven Varas correspond to the seven-day civil week and are traditionally associated with the Sun, Moon and visible planets: Ravi, Soma, Mangala, Budha, Guru, Shukra and Shani.

## Why does Vara exist?

Vara provides the weekly framework within which other Panchang elements and observances are interpreted. It is also used by traditional rules for festivals, Vratas, Muhurta and recurring time periods.

## How is Vara calculated?

Vara follows the local civil weekday. In Panchang presentation, the displayed Vara is tied to the local date and time convention used for the location.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditional practice associates each Vara with a planetary lord and particular observances. These associations can form one factor in selecting or understanding a time, but their application varies by tradition and purpose.

## How should Vara be used?

Use Vara as one contextual layer. For a specific activity, combine it with the relevant Tithi, Nakshatra, Muhurta or observance rule rather than treating the weekday alone as a complete decision.

## Common misconceptions

### Vara is the same as Tithi

No. Vara is the weekday; Tithi is a lunar calendrical interval.

### A weekday alone determines whether an activity is auspicious

Not generally. Traditional Muhurta and observance decisions can use several factors.
