# What is Yoga?

Yoga is one of the five principal limbs of Panchang. In this context it is a calendrical quantity derived from the combined ecliptic longitudes of the Sun and Moon. It should not be confused with the broader philosophical or physical meanings of the word yoga.

## Why does Yoga exist?

Yoga provides another way of describing the astronomical relationship of the Sun and Moon within the Panchanga framework. Traditional calendrical systems use the resulting Yoga in the classification of days and observances.

## How is Yoga calculated?

The geocentric ecliptic longitudes of the Sun and Moon are added. The resulting angle is divided into 27 equal sectors of 13°20′ each. The sector determines the current Panchanga Yoga, and its boundaries are calculated from the changing solar and lunar positions.

## Traditional interpretation

The 27 Yogas have traditional names and associations. Their practical use depends on the relevant calendrical or Muhurta tradition. Interpretive claims should be attributed to the tradition rather than presented as scientific effects.

## How should Yoga be used?

Use Yoga as one Panchanga factor. When a traditional rule calls for consideration of Yoga, check the exact local start and end time rather than relying on a fixed daily table.

## Common misconceptions

### This is the same Yoga as physical or spiritual yoga

No. Panchanga Yoga is an astronomical/calendrical quantity.

### Yoga alone determines the quality of a day

No. Panchang combines several limbs and observance rules can add further conditions.
