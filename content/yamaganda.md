# What is Yamaganda?

Yamaganda, also called Yamagandam, is a traditional daytime period that is commonly treated as unsuitable for beginning certain auspicious undertakings.

## Why does it exist?

It forms part of the traditional set of daytime intervals used when selecting time. It is normally considered alongside other Panchang timing factors rather than in isolation.

## How is Yamaganda calculated?

The commonly used method divides the local interval from sunrise to sunset into eight equal segments and assigns one segment to Yamaganda according to the weekday. Exact local clock times therefore change with sunrise, sunset and location.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditional practice generally recommends avoiding the commencement of specified auspicious activities during Yamaganda. Application varies by tradition and activity.

## How should it be used?

If selecting a time for an activity for which your tradition observes Yamaganda, check the local calculated interval and consider an alternative suitable period.

## Common misconceptions

### Yamaganda is a fixed clock time

No. It depends on the local daytime interval.

### Nothing can happen during Yamaganda

That is an overstatement. Traditional guidance concerns selected activities, especially their commencement.
