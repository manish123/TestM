# What is Nakshatra?

Nakshatra is a division of the ecliptic used in Indian astronomical and calendrical traditions. The commonly used system contains 27 Nakshatras, each spanning 13°20′ of the ecliptic.

## Why does Nakshatra exist?

Nakshatras provide a fine-grained lunar reference system. Panchang uses the Moon's position through this system to identify the Nakshatra operating at a given time and to support calendrical, observance and traditional timing rules.

## How is Nakshatra calculated?

The Moon's ecliptic longitude is mapped into successive 13°20′ sectors. The sector containing the Moon determines the current Nakshatra. Exact start and end times are calculated from the Moon's changing astronomical position and displayed in local civil time.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditional systems associate each Nakshatra with characteristic qualities, deities and uses. Applications differ between calendrical, Muhurta and Jyotisha contexts; those contexts should not be conflated.

## How should Nakshatra be used?

For Panchang use, identify the current Nakshatra and its local start/end time, then consult the specific rule for the observance or activity. Do not treat the Nakshatra label alone as a complete prediction.

## Common misconceptions

### There are only 27 stars

The term refers to divisions of the ecliptic, not simply a list of 27 physical stars.

### Nakshatra is the same as Tithi

No. Tithi is based on Sun-Moon angular separation; Nakshatra is based on the Moon's position along the ecliptic.
