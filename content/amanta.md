# Amanta (अमांत)

## 1. What Is It?

Amanta is a traditional lunar-month reckoning system in which the lunar month ends with Amavasya (New Moon). The following day, Shukla Pratipada, begins the next lunar month.

In this system, the month is organised as:

- Shukla Paksha — from Shukla Pratipada through Purnima.
- Krishna Paksha — from Krishna Pratipada through Amavasya.

The underlying astronomical tithi sequence is the same lunar cycle used by other lunar-month reckoning systems; Amanta specifies where the month boundary is placed.

## 2. Why Does It Exist?

The Amanta convention provides a systematic way of naming and organising lunar months for calendars, festivals, vratas and ritual observances.

The supplied source material also presents traditional interpretations connecting lunar cycles with biological, psychological and environmental influences. These belong to the traditional interpretive framework and should not be represented as established scientific mechanisms.

## 3. How Is It Calculated or Determined?

The underlying lunar phase is determined from the geocentric longitudinal separation of the Moon and Sun:

`D = (Moon longitude - Sun longitude) mod 360°`

Amavasya occurs when the separation reaches the conjunction boundary at 360°/0°. In the Amanta convention, that Amavasya boundary ends the current lunar month; the following Shukla Pratipada begins the next month.

The exact ending moment is location- and timezone-dependent because the astronomical event occurs at a particular instant and must be converted into local civil time.

Detailed regional rules for month naming are not sufficiently established by the available source material and should not be invented.

## 4. How Can a Normal Person Use It?

A person using an Amanta Panchang can use the system to understand the month name associated with a given date and to locate festivals, vratas and observances within the lunar calendar.

Important practical points:

- Shukla Paksha follows Amavasya and begins the new Amanta month.
- Krishna Paksha follows the Shukla half and leads to the next Amavasya.
- Festival and vrata determination may depend on the applicable tithi rule, local sunrise or other event-specific criteria; the month-reckoning system alone does not determine every observance.
- When comparing Panchangs, first establish whether they use Amanta or Purnimanta month naming.

## 5. Common Misconceptions

### Amanta changes the underlying tithis

It does not. Amanta is a month-reckoning convention. The astronomical tithi sequence remains the same.

### Amanta and Purnimanta are different lunar cycles

They are not different astronomical lunar cycles. They place the month boundary differently around the same sequence of lunar phases.

### A festival date can be determined from the month name alone

It cannot. Festival determination can involve tithi, sunrise, moonrise, sankranti and other traditional rules depending on the festival.

## 6. Important Distinctions

### Amanta vs Purnimanta

- Amanta: the month ends at Amavasya.
- Purnimanta: the month ends at Purnima.

The distinction primarily affects the naming of the lunar month, particularly the Krishna Paksha portion.

### Lunar Masa vs Solar Masa

A lunar masa is organised around the lunisolar tithi cycle. A solar masa is based on the Sun's transition from one zodiac sign to another (Sankramana).

### Amavasya vs Amanta

Amavasya is an astronomical/tithi boundary associated with the Sun-Moon conjunction. Amanta is the calendar convention that uses that boundary to end a lunar month.

## 7. Traditional Interpretation

Traditional sources associate lunar-month organisation with the broader Panchanga and ritual calendar system. The supplied material also contains explanations involving subtle or electromagnetic influences. These should be presented as traditional interpretations rather than established physical science.

Astronomical/calculational fact: Amanta is a calendrical convention whose month boundary is tied to the Amavasya/conjunction boundary of the lunar cycle.

## 8. Variations and Disagreements

Regional calendar traditions may differ in how lunar months are named and in how specific festivals and ritual observances are assigned to months.

The available source material does not establish a complete independent taxonomy of all regional Amanta naming variations. Those variations should therefore be added only when supported by additional authoritative material.
