# What is Ekadashi?

Ekadashi is the eleventh Tithi of each lunar fortnight. Because there are two Pakshas in a lunar month, Ekadashi generally occurs twice in each lunar month: one in Shukla Paksha and one in Krishna Paksha.

## Why does Ekadashi exist?

Ekadashi is both a calendrical marker and an important Vrat observance. Its dates are determined through the lunar Tithi system rather than by a fixed civil calendar date.

## How is Ekadashi calculated?

The underlying calculation begins with Tithi. A Panchang identifies when the eleventh Tithi begins and ends and then applies the relevant observance rule, which can involve sunrise and the local civil day. The exact fasting and Parana rules should be calculated for the user's location.

## Traditional interpretation

Ekadashi Vrat is traditionally associated with restraint, worship, fasting and devotion, with particularly strong importance in Vaishnava traditions. Rules concerning food, fasting and Parana vary between traditions.

## How should it be used?

Use a location-aware Panchang to identify the correct Ekadashi date and the relevant fasting/Parana timings for your tradition. Do not infer the date from a generic monthly list when precision matters.

## Common misconceptions

### Ekadashi is always on the same date every month

No. It follows Tithi rather than the civil calendar.

### Every tradition follows exactly the same Ekadashi rule

No. Observance and Parana conventions can vary.
