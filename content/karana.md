# What is Karana?

Karana is one of the five limbs of Panchang and represents half of a Tithi. There are 60 Karana occurrences in a lunar month, produced by dividing the 30 Tithis into two parts each.

## Why does Karana exist?

Karana gives Panchang a finer subdivision of the lunar day. Traditional calendrical rules use particular Karana names and classifications when determining observances and suitability of activities.

## How is Karana calculated?

A Tithi spans 12 degrees of Sun-Moon angular separation. Each Karana spans half of that interval, or 6 degrees. The sequence contains recurring Karana names together with four fixed Karana associated with specific positions in the lunar month.

## Traditional interpretation

Traditional Panchanga practice assigns different characteristics and uses to the Karana classifications. The relevance depends on the specific activity and tradition.

## How should Karana be used?

Treat Karana as a detailed Panchanga factor. If an observance or Muhurta rule specifically requires it, use the locally calculated Karana and its exact boundaries.

## Common misconceptions

### Karana is another name for Tithi

No. Karana is half of a Tithi.

### Every Karana has a unique occurrence in the month

No. Several Karana repeat according to a defined sequence, while four are fixed.
